================================================================================
LTESniffer — UL (uplink) capture investigation: findings, open questions, solution
Date: 2026-07-15
================================================================================

THE SYMPTOM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Dual-B210 sniffer decodes the downlink fine (hundreds–thousands of MAC frames per
run) but only ~5–7 uplink frames land in the pcap, even in a "dense" cell where
the eNB schedules THOUSANDS of UL grants (5000+ per 40 s run). The few UL frames
we do get are almost all RRCConnectionReestablishmentRequest / CCCH (Msg3) — the
single most robust uplink message that exists. We essentially never capture normal
scheduled UL user data.

Network under test: live commercial Cellcom Israel macro cell (MCC 425 / MNC 02,
TAC 913, eNB id 14384), 4G with n78 5G redirect. This is a REAL macro cell with
many real UEs, not a lab eNB (confirmed: 149 distinct C-RNTIs + rich SIB neighbor
lists in a short capture). Working band: DL 1825 / UL 1730 (cell 217/238) and the
"dense" cell PCI 83 on DL 1845 / UL 1750.


WHAT WE RULED OUT (each with direct measurement, not theory)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. NOT a pcap-writing bug.
   write_ul_crnti() fires unconditionally on every CRC success. Measured the live
   decode-success counter vs the actual pcap UL-frame count on many runs — they
   MATCH EXACTLY every time (2=2, 3=3, 6=6, 23=23). Every UL frame that decodes is
   written. The pcap is faithful.

2. NOT aggressive filtering.
   The only pre-decode gate (investigate_valid_ul_grant) rejects structurally
   invalid grants only (rnti=0, TBS=0 in both tables, invalid PRB count). There is
   NO SNR/power threshold discarding weak-but-real grants.

3. NOT a radio-sync bug (this WAS broken and is now FIXED — see below).

4. NOT a timing / FFT-window bug.
   Took 361 real failed-grant raw-IQ captures and swept the FFT window across
   +/-1024 samples (+/-44 us — far beyond the cyclic prefix and the whole
   DL-UL delay spread) through the REAL srsran decoder, offline, deterministically.
   Result: ZERO grants recovered CRC, ZERO reached even 6 dB SNR at ANY window
   position. Timing is not the bottleneck for these grants.

5. The "GUI shows 118 UL but pcap has 4" confusion: NOT a bug, a mislabel.
   The GUI "UL data" tile counted UL GRANTS SCHEDULED (DCI-0 assignments seen in
   the downlink), not UL frames captured. Fixed the GUI to show real captured
   counts from the pcap alongside the grant counts.


WHAT WE FIXED (real bugs found and corrected)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. THE RASPBERRY-PI 1-PPS WAS DEAD. (root cause of the old "sync" failures)
   The Pi's pps.service was green in systemctl but its own log showed, on EVERY
   pulse forever: "pps.sh: Can't set pin value, not an output". The bash bit-bang
   loop set GPIO18 to output once, then toggled it with SEPARATE pinctrl processes
   whose output-mode did not persist -> every write hit an input pin. GPIO18 read
   permanently low. The USRPs received ZERO 1-PPS pulses, ever.
   FIX: wrote /usr/local/bin/pps_hw.c (in repo: scripts/rpi/pps_hw.c) — a single
   long-lived process that holds GPIO18 as output, writes edges by direct BCM
   register poke, and schedules every edge from a fixed epoch with
   clock_nanosleep(TIMER_ABSTIME) so the 1 Hz period never drifts. Installed on the
   Pi (pps.service now runs pps_start.sh which compiles+runs it). Verified with a
   new tool build/ppscheck: the two B210s now latch the SAME 1-PPS edge — sub-second
   skew collapsed from ~39,800 us to < 1 ms.

B. THE DUAL-RADIO REALIGN WAS PERMANENTLY DORMANT.
   The recv-wrapper code that aligns the UL radio to the DL radio was gated on
   time_sec_a == time_sec_b, which was NEVER true (the radios latched different PPS
   edges, ~2 s apart). So the alignment never ran and the UL sample window was at a
   random per-run offset.
   FIX: rewrote the gate (LTESniffer_Core.cc) to align on the WRAP-AWARE FRACTIONAL
   skew regardless of the integer-second offset. Result (measured live): the realign
   now fires once and the A-B skew locks to ~29 NANOSECONDS and holds for the whole
   run. The two radios are now genuinely sample-locked.

   NOTE: fixes A+B were necessary and correct, but they did NOT by themselves make
   the UL yield jump — because the remaining limit is signal level (see below).


THE CURRENT BEST-EVIDENCE EXPLANATION: THE UL WAS UNDER-RANGED IN THE ADC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Definitive offline test: replayed the actual FAILING low-MCS grants (QPSK MCS 0/2,
which decode at ~-2..0 dB in theory) through the real decoder with a wide timing
sweep. Even at the BEST window they reach only ~1–2 dB SNR and never decode.

Then measured the raw IQ level: the UL signal was peaking at only 0.36% of the
ADC full-scale range — i.e. ~49 dB of UNUSED headroom — while the UL gain was set
to only 30 dB WITH NO LNA. The signal was sitting almost at the bottom of the ADC,
buried near the ADC quantization / instrument noise floor. THAT is what was pinning
the effective SNR at ~1–2 dB — not the airlink itself. (The DL radio is properly
ranged, which is why DL decodes fine; this is UL-specific.)

Evidence that this is (at least partly) the throttle — raising UL gain helped SNR:
    UL gain 30 dB : ADC 0.4% full-scale, UL SNR max 3.24 dB, mean 2.32 dB
    UL gain 60 dB : ADC 2.4% full-scale, UL SNR max 5.50 dB, mean 3.00 dB   (+2.3 dB)
    UL gain 70 dB : ADC 7.0% full-scale, UL SNR max 4.91 dB (quiet window, fewer UEs)
Peak SNR rose ~2 dB purely from lifting the signal out of the instrument noise
floor. The gain-70 run happened to hit a quiet/short traffic window (only ~2000
grants vs ~5000), so its lower decode count is NOT a fair comparison — the SNR
trend is the meaningful signal.

INTERPRETATION: we were partly instrument-noise-limited (fixable with gain / LNA)
and are now approaching the antenna-thermal-noise limit. The airlink SNR of these
particular UEs at our antenna is genuinely marginal because this is a passive
monitor of a live macro cell — real subscriber phones are far away and power-
control their uplink to reach the TOWER, not us, so their UL arrives tens of dB
weaker than the (close, high-power) downlink.


WHAT TO CHECK NEXT (in priority order)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. FINISH THE GAIN SWEEP. We are at 7% ADC with ~23 dB headroom still unused.
   Try UL gain 74–76 (B210 max is ~76 dB) in a BUSY traffic window and compare UL
   SNR + decode count against gain 60 on a similar-density window. Watch for
   overload (peak approaching 100% / clipping). This tells us where the
   instrument-noise-vs-antenna-noise crossover is.

2. ADD AN LNA ON THE UL (physical, highest-impact if the front end is the limit).
   A low-noise amplifier at the UL antenna raises the signal before the B210's own
   noise figure degrades it — the correct way to lift a genuinely weak UL. Pair it
   with a UL-band filter to avoid out-of-band overload. (Config hint already in the
   GUI: "no LNA -> 50, with LNA -> 30".) NOTE: earlier LNA experiments were
   inconclusive/oscillating — use a proper UL-band LNA + filter and verify with the
   ADC-utilization + SNR check we built.

3. IMPROVE UL ANTENNA / GEOMETRY. Get the UL antenna higher / clearer / closer to
   where the UEs are. This is the fundamental lever for a passive UL monitor.

4. BETTER CHANNEL ESTIMATOR (software, uncertain gain, only worth it once ranged).
   The current chest is LS + 3-tap smoothing. For the tiny 2-RB grants at marginal
   SNR the channel estimate is noisy. A DFT/delay-domain denoiser or LMMSE could
   squeeze a few more marginal grants once the signal is properly ranged. We have
   the offline replay harness (build/src/ul_iq_replay) and 600 captured grants to
   test any estimator deterministically before touching the live decoder.

5. VERIFY DMRS CONFIG on strong grants (rule out a subtle config bug). The
   cyclic-shift sweep (UL_DMRS_DIAG) could not fire because the grants were too weak
   to clear even a -20 dB power gate. Re-run it once the gain is up and grants are
   stronger: if a NON-signaled DMRS cyclic shift gives markedly higher SNR than the
   signaled one, our DMRS extraction is wrong (a real, fixable bug). Currently
   UNVERIFIED because there was no strong grant to test it on.

6. THE DECISIVE EXPERIMENT — CONTROLLED UE. Put a phone with a Cellcom SIM close to
   the UL antenna, camped on this cell, doing a sustained large UPLOAD, and capture.
   A strong, close, known UL removes all ambiguity:
     - if it decodes well (real DTCH/DCCH data, not just CCCH) -> the whole chain
       works and the on-air limit is purely geometry (distant UEs). DONE.
     - if even a strong close UE only yields CCCH -> there is a deeper UL decode
       issue and we chase the PUSCH path with a signal we KNOW is strong.


WHAT I THINK THE PROBLEM AND SOLUTION ARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM: There is no single software bug swallowing the uplink. The pcap, filtering,
and (after our fixes) the PPS sync and dual-radio alignment are all correct. The UL
yield is limited by SIGNAL LEVEL / SNR at the receiver:
  (a) an instrument-side throttle we already started removing — the UL was grossly
      under-ranged in the ADC (0.4% full-scale at 30 dB gain, no LNA), which held
      the effective SNR ~2 dB below where it should be; and
  (b) a fundamental airlink limit — as a passive monitor of a live macro cell, we
      hear distant subscribers' power-controlled uplink tens of dB weaker than the
      downlink, so only the most robust messages (Msg3/CCCH) clear the decoder.

SOLUTION (in order of expected impact):
  1. RANGE THE UL PROPERLY. Keep raising UL gain toward ~74 dB and add a UL-band
     LNA + filter so the signal sits in a healthy part of the ADC (roughly
     10–30% full-scale) instead of 0.4%. This is the cheapest, highest-leverage
     fix and it is already showing gains (+2.3 dB SNR from 30->60 dB).
  2. IMPROVE UL RECEPTION GEOMETRY (antenna placement / height / direction).
  3. THEN, if still short, a better channel estimator (DFT/LMMSE) to squeeze the
     marginal grants — test offline first on the 600 captured grants.
  4. To PROVE the chain end-to-end and separate geometry from any residual decode
     issue: the controlled-UE upload test.

Confidence: HIGH that it is not a pcap/filter/sync/timing bug (all measured).
MEDIUM-HIGH that ADC under-ranging + true airlink weakness together explain it.
The controlled-UE test (#6 above) is what would make it 100% certain.


PER-UE TIMING — "how do we capture all UEs like the real eNB does?" (2026-07-15b)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PHYSICS (why a passive sniffer is NOT automatically like the eNB):
  - The eNB runs a closed loop: it measures each UE's round-trip delay and
    commands a Timing Advance (TA) so the UE transmits EARLY by exactly 2x its
    propagation delay. Result: every UE's UL arrives ALIGNED at the eNB's subframe
    boundary, within the cyclic prefix. The eNB uses ONE FFT window for everyone.
  - We are passive and at a DIFFERENT location. TA is computed for the eNB, not us.
    Each UE's UL arrives at OUR antenna with a residual offset = (d_us - d_enb)/c
    that depends on that UE's geometry. Measured spread at our site: about +/-10 us,
    with each UE having its OWN roughly-stable offset (confirmed: repeat grants from
    the same RNTI cluster tightly; different RNTIs sit at different offsets, e.g.
    -4.8 to +7.1 us). ~1/3 of UEs land OUTSIDE the normal CP (~4.7 us).
  - A grant whose window is off by more than the CP loses orthogonality (ISI/ICI)
    and CANNOT decode at ANY SNR until the window is re-centred. So a single global
    window (what the code effectively used) structurally throws away ~1/3 of UEs.

THE CODE GAP (found this session):
  - srsRAN's timing estimate res->ta_us is computed by srsran_vec_estimate_frequency
    on the DMRS pilots — i.e. it is a PHASE-SLOPE estimate. A phase slope is only
    meaningful WITHIN the CP; once the window is off by more than a CP the pilots are
    corrupted by ISI and the slope is garbage. So ta_us CANNOT tell us how to reach a
    beyond-CP UE (chicken-and-egg: you need the window roughly right to measure how
    wrong it is). The old offset-retry used ta_us + a +/-64-sample (+/-2.8 us)
    fallback — far too narrow for a +/-10 us spread, and mis-steered for beyond-CP UEs.

THE FIX BUILT (software per-UE Timing Advance; the eNB-equivalent for a sniffer):
  UL_Sniffer_PUSCH.cc — for each grant that carries real energy (chest-FREE
  allocated-RB power >= UL_TIMING_ACQ_MINDB above the noise floor), do a WIDE
  two-stage window search: coarse scan +/-UL_TIMING_ACQ_MAX samples (chest SNR as
  the metric, which peaks when the window is centred on THAT UE), then a fine scan
  around the peak, then decode at the peak window. This re-centres the FFT window
  per-UE — the software equivalent of the per-UE TA the eNB commands — so we can
  capture UEs whose UL lands outside the CP.
  RT-SAFE: the search is ENERGY-GATED, so only the ~1% of grants that actually carry
  signal pay for it; the ~99% noise grants are untouched (a blind wide sweep on every
  grant drops ~11% of subframes — measured — and must never be used).
  Env: UL_TIMING_ACQ=1 (on), UL_TIMING_ACQ_MAX=300 (samples, ~13 us each side),
  UL_TIMING_ACQ_MINDB=3. OFF by default; wired into the GUI Dense Test.

HONEST CAVEAT: per-UE timing is NECESSARY (it's the only way to capture beyond-CP
UEs) but it is not SUFFICIENT at this site right now. Offline replay of the best
gain-70 grants shows they cap at ~1-2 dB SNR even at the perfect window and gain
only 0.2-1.7 dB from re-centring — because the underlying signal is marginal
(distant UEs + still-imperfect ADC ranging). Timing acquisition PAYS OFF only once a
UE has adequate SNR at the right window. So it combines with the gain/LNA/geometry
work: gain gets the UE above the noise floor; per-UE timing puts the window where
that UE actually is. The controlled-UE test (strong, close, known timing) is the
clean way to SEE the window-shift recover a beyond-CP UE.


GEOMETRY COMPUTED FROM REAL COORDINATES (2026-07-15c) — decisive
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cell PCI 83 (TAC 19421): 34.821972, 32.146083
Our sniffer:             34.815186, 32.142839
Distance tower<->us  d(E,S) = 734 m  (one-way light time 2.45 us).

HARD BOUND: the max possible UL arrival offset for ANY UE at our receiver is
    |Δ|_max = 2·d(E,S)/c = 4.89 us  (= 113 samples @ 23.04 Msps ≈ ONE cyclic prefix)
No UE — however far from the tower — can be offset more than this from our DL
reference, because Δ = [d(U,S) − d(E,U) − d(E,S)]/c and the worst case is bounded
by 2·d(E,S)/c (derived from the triangle inequality; TA = 2·d(E,U)/c cancels).

CONSEQUENCES:
1. Geometry is FAVORABLE. At only 734 m from the tower the per-UE timing spread is
   at most ~1 CP. We are NOT in a "distant tower, huge timing spread" regime. A
   correctly-centred window with at most a ~1-CP (±113-sample) shift covers every
   UE. The blind ±13 us (±300-sample) search was massive overkill (and caused the
   RT overload).
2. The ±10 us "spread" we measured in the DL-UL delay is therefore NOT real per-UE
   geometry — physics forbids >4.9 us. It is dominated by low-SNR estimation noise
   (srsRAN's phase-slope ta_us scatters badly at 2-3 dB) plus any fixed nominal-
   window offset. ~38% of measured delays exceed the 4.9 us geometric bound =
   noise, not signal.
3. This REINFORCES that SNR — not timing — is the binding constraint. Being only
   734 m out, if the UL signal were strong, a near-nominal window would already
   capture most UEs. It doesn't -> the signal is too weak (marginal SNR), exactly
   the ADC-ranging / airlink-level story.

REVISED TIMING PLAN (given the geometry):
  - Replace the blind/energy-gated WIDE search with a NARROW per-UE centring:
    at most ±113 samples (±4.9 us), which is cheap and RT-safe.
  - TA-DIRECTED sizing: we already decode the RAR Timing Advance
    (DL_Sniffer_PDSCH.cc:626, get_ta_cmd). Per UE, the offset bound is
    −min(TA, 2·d(E,S)/c) with a KNOWN sign (UL arrives at/He before our DL ref),
    so a UE with small TA needs an even tinier search. This makes per-UE centring
    both cheap and correct.
  - But this is a minor correction. The primary lever remains SNR: gain/LNA to
    lift the UE above the noise floor, and — powerfully — getting even CLOSER to
    the tower shrinks 2·d(E,S)/c further (at the tower, spread -> 0, we ARE the eNB)
    AND raises received UL level.


TWO MORE SOFTWARE LEVERS TESTED AND EXHAUSTED (2026-07-15d)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DMRS cyclic-shift config bug — RULED OUT. Ran the 8-way cyclic-shift SNR sweep on
25,343 UL grants (UL_DMRS_DIAG_PWR=-100). If our DMRS extraction were wrong, the
peak SNR would sit at ONE consistent non-signaled shift. Instead the peak is
SCATTERED across all 8 shifts (histogram ~flat: 21% at the signaled shift — above
the 12.5% chance level, so the config is RIGHT — the rest scattered by noise). Even
on high-RB-power grants (rawRBpow>=6 dB, n=637) the best of all 8 shifts reaches
only ~0.8 dB median. So the high-energy-but-no-DMRS grants are NOT a config bug:
that energy does not carry a correlatable PUSCH DMRS at any shift => it is
interference / another UE / FALSE DCI detections (blind DCI search false positives),
not real target-UE PUSCH we are mis-handling. (Implication: the "thousands of UL
grants" over-counts real grants; our decode RATE on genuine grants is better than
the raw ratio suggests.)

Channel-estimator denoising — TESTED, marginal, insufficient. Added an override to
ul_iq_replay (UL_CHEST_SMOOTH=len, Gaussian). On 20 marginal QPSK grants:
    smooth 3 (default): avg chest SNR -0.43 dB, decoded 0/20
    smooth 5          : avg chest SNR +0.29 dB, decoded 0/20   (+0.7 dB, no decodes)
    smooth 9 / 15     : WORSE (-1.7 / -3.5 dB) — over-smoothing biases a tiny
                        allocation whose channel varies across the RBs.
A modest 3->5 tap bump buys ~0.7 dB but does NOT cross the decode threshold, and
heavier smoothing hurts. The estimator lever is essentially exhausted.

FINAL VERDICT (multiply confirmed, every independent method):
  The captured UL at this site/time is SNR-LIMITED at ~1-2 dB. That decodes only the
  most robust low-MCS/CCCH messages; higher-MCS grants (which need 8-15 dB) cannot
  and should not decode. NO software change decodes a 1 dB grant. Ruled out with
  data: pcap-write, filtering, sync, timing (geometry <=1 CP; wide sweep recovers
  nothing), DMRS config, channel-estimator, ADC ranging (fixed 0.4%->7%, now
  antenna-noise-limited). The remaining levers are PHYSICAL:
    1. UL LNA (+ UL-band filter): lowers system noise figure — the correct fix for
       an antenna-noise-limited receiver; directly raises effective SNR.
    2. Closer to / in line of sight with the tower: raises received UL level AND
       shrinks the timing spread (2·d/c). We are 734 m out; closer is strictly
       better on both axes.
    3. Better / higher UL antenna.
  Software is at its limit for this signal level. The controlled-UE test (strong,
  close, known UE uploading) is the clean way to prove the full chain works and
  that the on-air shortfall is purely received-signal level.
